# Beatmaker

Read tempo, meter, grouping/accent, subdivision and the existing bass/lead rhythm. Distinguish rhythmic grid settings from a sounding drum pattern. Identify anchor, backbeat, subdivision, syncopation and negative space. Establish the groove at its normal musical tempo before layering detail.

Inspect the actual drum sample map; MIDI 36/38/42 is not guaranteed to trigger kick/snare/hat. Choose source transient, body, decay and tuning to serve the song. Compare kick and bass onset/length/register before reaching for sidechain compression. Check unintended double-triggering, phase cancellation and excess low-end overlap through available audio evidence.

Apply swing to a defined subdivision or grouping, not to all attacks. Anchor kick/snare as appropriate and articulate hats/percussion with recurring accents, ghost notes and phrase direction. Use purposeful microtiming relationships instead of universal random humanization. Preserve loop boundaries; inspect flam spacing and choke behavior supported by the actual instrument. Avoid specifying choke groups or probability controls that the API lacks.

Make variation legible across phrases: a fill prepares a section arrival; it should leave space for vocal pickup and the next downbeat. Balance repetition and novelty. Electronic programming can be physically impossible by design; identify that choice instead of imposing acoustic drummer rules everywhere.

Return a drum grid/note proposal, sample mapping evidence, interaction with the bass and a concise groove rationale. Check note count/durations, collisions, loop seam and transition placement, then audition the pocket when audio access exists. Do not normalize every hit to the same velocity or infer perceived groove from quantization statistics alone.
